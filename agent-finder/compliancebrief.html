Data Sourcing & Compliance

Before the match engine reads a single agent, one question shapes the build: which sources can we actually use? This brief lays out the options, the risk behind each, and what we'd still need to verify — so the sourcing call gets made with eyes open, not by default.

For Connor Altizer · Senior Partner From Legal & Technical Research Date June 23, 2026 Re Sourcing options for the agent database
Where we're leaning
Toward a permissioned V1

A curated, source-linked build looks like the most defensible path today. Read it as the starting direction to pressure-test — a strong recommendation, not a locked ruling.

What's still open
The reads can move

Everything below is current legal-research reading, not a final call. A license, updated terms, or counsel sign-off could shift any source from red toward green — flag anything that looks off.

What would change the call
  • Which directories would license data to us, rather than sit at a flat No-Go.
  • Counsel sign-off on the terms-of-service reads below before any of them gate production.
  • Whether the curated V1 set is 50 or 150 agents — and who owns keeping it fresh.

Source by source

The strongest signal comes from the platforms' own terms. Verdicts are a working read to steer sourcing — filter by posture, and treat each as a decision input to verify, not a rule already in force.

Citations [S1]–[S8] are review notes, not yet independently re-verified. Treat these verdicts as guidance to inform the sourcing decision — they should pass a citation audit and counsel review before any of them gate production.

03 — Risk reads

The four questions to settle

Each is a judgment call with a confidence level attached — not a settled fact. They're here to focus the discussion and show where the exposure actually sits, so we can weigh it rather than guess.

Question 1 · TOS / contractHigh concern

Legal & TOS risk looks high

MSWL, Reedsy, Duotrope and Publishers Marketplace each carry terms that read as hostile to automated extraction or commercial reuse. That points away from a broad scrape as the default — worth a closer legal look before ruling it in or out.

Question 2 · FreshnessLikely

Freshness is hard to hold at scale

"Unsustainable" is too absolute for a narrow tool — but a broad DB claiming reliable status, URLs and emails decays fast. The mitigation: give every volatile field a source, a date, and a TTL.

Question 3 · CFAA / defamationMaterial

CFAA probably isn't the main risk

Van Buren and hiQ v. LinkedIn suggest public scraping isn't automatically illegal. The exposure that looks more real: contract breach, paywall/login circumvention, database claims, and defamation from shared negative notes.

Question 4 · StrategySupported

Favor curation over broad scraping

The direction the evidence supports: a permission-aware research assistant — permitted/licensed sources, manual curation, field-level freshness, a source-rights register, and no copied expressive text.

04 — Freshness (proposed)

Field-level TTLs

A static scraped database goes stale within weeks. The proposed rule: every volatile field carries a time-to-live and a required control before the match engine trusts it. Tune the numbers — these are starting points to argue with, not gospel.

05 — Product direction

What we'd lean into, what we'd skip

Steer away from

  • Bulk scrapers over agent directories
  • Copied or paraphrased profile prose at scale
  • Stored paywalled deal data (Publishers Marketplace)
  • Extracted user comments, reports, private query stats
  • Republished negative notes about agents or agencies
  • An "always current" comprehensive-database claim

Lean into

  • A curated start set of 50–150 agents
  • Official agency pages as the primary source
  • Only factual fields, each with a source link
  • "Last verified" + "verify before submitting" warnings
  • User-private notes that are never published
  • A permission queue + agent correction / opt-out
