The Issues — 12 items, highest priority first
01

The contract isn't signed yet — use that

Critical
Willow Floors already signed on June 10. Tom has not. That ordering is actually good news: every change below is far easier to get before a signature and a deposit than after. Once Tom signs and the 40% is paid, leverage drops sharply. The single most important move is to treat the items in this document as a short list to settle first, then sign.
What to do
Don't sign or pay anything yet. Send the requested changes (a ready-to-send email is at the bottom of this page), get them in writing, then sign.
02

Product is now identified, but the contract still does not match it

HighBoth reviews
What the contract says
"…approximately 935 square feet of Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP) flooring, 5.2 mm thickness with a 22 mil wear layer…"
The selected floor is now known: EF Legendary, color 6005 Orthodox, 7" x 48", 5.2 mm rigid SPC core with a 20 mil wear layer and attached 1.0 mm IXPE pad. Two follow-ups remain. First, the contract still does not name it, so a look-alike could still be substituted. Second, the contract states a 22 mil wear layer but the actual product is 20 mil. The Legendary line is offered in 12 mil and 20 mil wear layers and 22 mil is not a listed option, which confirms the contract figure is an error. 20 mil is a durable premium-residential / light-commercial grade, so this is not a quality downgrade, but the written spec and the chosen product disagree.
Ask for
Name this exact product in the contract (brand, collection, color, size, 5.2 mm / 20 mil) and correct the wear-layer figure from 22 mil to 20 mil so the spec matches what is being installed.
03

No completion date

HighBoth reviews
What the contract says
"This Contract will begin on the Effective Date and shall remain in effect until completion of the Services…"
There is a start date but no end date, no number of working days, and no maximum duration. "Until completion" gives the contractor unlimited time with no remedy if the job drags. For a roughly 935 sq ft single-product install, this is normally a few days of work.
Ask for
"Work to be substantially completed within ___ calendar days of start," unless delayed by an approved change order or force majeure.
04

Weak warranty + only a 30-day window to report defects

HighBoth reviews
What the contract says
"…if the Owner identifies and notifies the Contractor any deficiencies within 30 days of completion of the Contract."  The Warranty section otherwise only promises a generic "workmanlike manner" standard — no stated length.
Flooring problems frequently appear after 30 days: planks separating, edges lifting, hollow or bouncy spots, quarter-round pulling away as the room's humidity cycles. A 30-day reporting window can expire before the most common defects even show up. There is no stated labor-warranty length at all. The selected EF product carries a strong manufacturer material warranty (lifetime residential, lifetime waterproof, lifetime pet stains). That covers the flooring itself, but it is not a labor warranty, so a contractor workmanship warranty (target 1 year) is still needed on top of it.
Ask for
A 1-year workmanship warranty on labor and installation, plus pass-through of the manufacturer's material warranty — and language that the warranty period (not the 30 days) governs defect correction.
Legal note for Veronica
The "Correction of Work" clause and the Warranty clause work against each other here: a broad workmanship standard is undercut by a hard 30-day notice cap. Worth deciding whether to tie correction obligations to the warranty period instead, and whether NJ's implied warranty of workmanlike performance adds a backstop regardless of the written term.
05

Subfloor repairs are undefined and open-ended

HighBoth reviews
What the contract says
"Perform minor subfloor repairs in up to three (3) identified sections… Any additional concealed subfloor damage discovered after removal… will be considered additional work and will require customer approval before proceeding."
Two soft spots here. First, a "section" is never defined — square footage, dimensions, material, or repair method. Second, concealed damage is open-ended extra work at an unstated price, discovered only after the old floor is up, when stopping is least convenient. Requiring approval is good, but with no agreed rate the approval happens under pressure. This is the classic change-order trap.
Ask for
Define what's included (square footage or dimensions per section), and lock a capped or per-square-foot rate for any concealed repairs found on removal, so a surprise can't become a blank check.
06

License and insurance not verified in the document

HighBoth reviews
What the contract says
"Upon request, the Contractor will provide evidence of appropriate insurance…"  No NJ Home Improvement Contractor registration number and no 3-day cancellation notice appear anywhere.
New Jersey requires most home-improvement contractors to be registered with the state (a "HIC" number) and to include certain consumer protections. Insurance is only promised "upon request" rather than proven up front. None of this means Willow Floors is doing anything wrong — but it should be verified on paper before money changes hands, not afterward.
Ask for
The NJ HIC registration number and certificates of insurance (general liability + workers' comp) delivered before the deposit.
Legal note for Veronica
NJ's Consumer Fraud Act / Home Improvement Practices regs (N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16) require HIC registration and a written 3-day right to cancel on home-improvement contracts. Neither appears in this document. Worth confirming current registration status and whether the missing cancellation notice is material.
07

No acclimation or manufacturer-spec install language

MediumAlt review
Most LVP manufacturers require the planks to acclimate to the room and, in some cases, a moisture check on the subfloor before installation. Skipping it is a common cause of the lifting and gapping problems noted above — and skipping it can also void the material warranty. The contract says nothing about following the manufacturer's instructions.
Ask for
"Installation performed per the manufacturer's written requirements, including acclimation and moisture testing where required."
08

No cleanup or disposal standard

MediumBoth reviews
The contract never says the crew will haul away the old flooring, remove debris, or leave the house broom-clean. Disposal of the torn-out floor is also not clearly anyone's job. Usually a contractor handles this, but "usually" isn't in writing here.
Ask for
Removal and disposal of old flooring and debris, with the home left broom-clean on completion.
09

Furniture-moving scope is vague

MediumAlt review
What the contract says
"Remove and reinstall furniture as reasonably necessary…"  Specialty heavy items (pianos, safes, aquariums, pool tables, unlisted appliances) are explicitly excluded.
"Reasonably necessary" is subjective and a likely day-of disagreement. It's worth knowing in advance exactly what the crew moves versus what the family needs to clear themselves — beds, dressers, china cabinets, washer/dryer, refrigerator.
Ask for
A short written list of what the crew will move and what the homeowner must clear beforehand.
10

Payment method changes the price

MediumPractical
What the contract says
"Accepted payment methods: Cash, Credit card (2.99% per transaction), Bank transfer (1% per transaction), Personal Check (no fee)."
On a $6,021.43 job, paying by credit card adds about $180, and bank transfer about $60. A personal check is free. Nothing to negotiate here — just a choice that saves real money.
Recommendation
Pay the deposit and the balance by personal check.
11

Mandatory binding arbitration

Low–HighReviews disagree
What the contract says
"Any controversies or disputes… shall be resolved by binding arbitration in accordance with the then-current Commercial Arbitration Rules of the American Arbitration Association. The arbitrator(s) shall not have the authority to… award punitive damages."
This clause waives the right to go to normal court or a jury, limits appeals, and bars punitive damages. It's the most owner-unfriendly section in the contract. The two reviews split on how hard to push: one treats it as the single biggest legal concern and would try to strike it; the other rates it low because AAA Commercial arbitration can cost more than a $6,000 dispute is worth, and contractors rarely agree to remove it — so pushing hard risks the easier wins. A practical middle path satisfies both: don't fight to delete it, just carve out small claims.
Ask for
Preserve small-claims court for both parties (e.g., "either party may bring a claim within the jurisdictional limit of small-claims court"), or strike arbitration entirely if they'll agree.
Legal note for Veronica
This is the clause where your judgment should override the priority label. Points worth weighing: AAA Commercial (vs. Consumer) rules and fee schedule on a ~$6k matter; the punitive-damages and modification carve-outs; interaction with the attorneys'-fees "prevailing party" clause; and whether a small-claims carve-out is the right ask versus deletion. Your call on whether this is a deal-shaper or a live-with-it.
12

Drafting errors in the Termination clause

LowPractical
What the contract says
"…with or without cause, upon 30 to the other party… Upon Early Termination, the Provider shall receive a pro-rated payment…"
Two sloppy spots. "Upon 30 to the other party" is missing a word (almost certainly "30 days' written notice"). And "Provider" appears where the contract's defined party is "Contractor." Neither is dangerous, but a contract with blanks like this should be tightened before signing, and it's a free ask to fold in with the others.
Ask for
Correct to "upon 30 days' written notice," and change "Provider" to "Contractor."
