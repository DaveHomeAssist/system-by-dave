      01 · Executive Summary

      A kitchen update that triggers a service upgrade

      Converting the kitchen from a single gas range to an induction cooktop and electric wall oven adds electrical load to a panel that is already full and aged. Combined with a planned garage subpanel and a possible future hot tub, the home outgrows its 100A service.

      Status200A upgrade required
      Calculated load~77A kitchen → ~103A all loads
      Target budget$12K – $15K
      Next stepGather contractor quotes

      The Goal

      Replace the gas range with a separate induction cooktop and convection wall oven, refresh the microwave, and cap the gas line, all within the existing kitchen footprint.

      The Finding

      Service is confirmed 100A and the panel is full. Kitchen loads calculate to ~77A; with the garage and hot tub the load reaches ~103A, past the 100A service.

      The Approach

      Treat this as one 200A service upgrade serving three needs at once, sized with spare capacity so future work needs no second upgrade.

      Project Drivers

      Kitchen Modernization

      Induction cooktop and convection oven replace the dated gas range.

      Garage Subpanel

      Planned workshop, lighting, and yard circuits add load to the main service.

      Hot Tub Readiness

      A possible future hot tub is a large continuous load to plan for now.

      Aging Infrastructure

      Full 100A panel with mixed-brand breakers and condition concerns.

      A 200A service upgrade is required.Capacity, panel condition, and planned future loads all point to the same conclusion.

      How to read this budget

      • Planning ranges, not quotes. Final numbers come from three contractor bids per trade; treat the target band as the realistic landing zone.
      • The 200A upgrade is required, not optional. The panel is full and the combined kitchen, garage, and hot-tub loads exceed the 100A service, so it is a baseline line item.
      • Biggest swing factors: the appliance tier (Good vs Best is roughly $5,000) and cabinetry plus venting inside Installation. These move the total more than anything else.
      • Total vs target. The $8K–$21K total spans worst case to best case; the $12K–$15K target assumes the Better tier and typical install work.

      06 · DIY vs Licensed Trades

      Who does what

      Keep the hazardous, permit-bound work with licensed trades; do the safe, money-saving tasks in-house.

      Safe for Homeowner
      • Measure the range opening, counter depth, and oven cabinet.
      • Research and choose appliances; record plate amperage.
      • Order appliances and schedule delivery and haul-away.
      • Pull the old range out, only after the gas is capped.
      • Gather three quotes per trade; act as permit applicant.
      • Patching, painting, and trim after the trades finish.
      Licensed Trades Only
      • Cap the gas line — licensed plumber, no DIY.
      • 200A service upgrade and all main-panel work.
      • New 240V circuits and breakers at the panel.
      • Utility coordination, permits, and inspections.

      Gas and main-panel electrical require licensed trades, permits, and inspection.
