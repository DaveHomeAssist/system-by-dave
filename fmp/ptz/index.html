01 / Rules

Two controllers, two camera families.

Open catwalk check-in →

Before doors, confirm which CAM button selects the house PTZ, which preset is the safe wide, and what Camera 4 is expected to cover. Once the show starts, operate the camera and leave picture engineering to the video office.

Who does what

The SuperJoy drives Camera 4. The video office handles camera engineering.

Operate framing, pan, tilt, zoom, focus and approved presets. Do not change video format, exposure, white balance, gain, color, network settings or camera-control configuration unless the Head of Video specifically asks you to.

If the picture looks wrong, call video. Do not try to engineer around it from the camera menus.

02 / Before doors

Select, prove, set.

SuperJoy manual ↗
01 · Check in

Know the assignment

  • Camera 4 is the house P240 on switcher input 4.
  • Confirm which SuperJoy CAM button selects it.
  • Confirm the safe-wide preset and the shots the director expects from Camera 4.
  • Confirm any restricted angles or movement limits.
  • Known issue: FMP-8. For now, treat Camera 4 as preset-first and do not live-track performers unless the Head of Video clears it.
  • If the tour adds PTZ cameras, make sure you know which controller buttons belong to the house camera before the show.
02 · Select

Select Camera 4

  1. Power up the SuperJoy and confirm the controller is responsive.
  2. Select the CAM button assigned to the house P240. If the wrong camera moves, stop and call video.
  3. Confirm 4 PTZ has picture on the multiview.
  4. With Camera 4 off program, make a small pan, tilt and zoom move to prove control.
  5. Do not press HOME as a substitute for the safe-wide preset. HOME is a camera function; the FMP safe wide is a show preset.
03 · Prove

Prove the moves you need

  • Pan left and right. It should start and stop cleanly.
  • Tilt up and down. Same.
  • Zoom in and out and check focus at the useful show distances.
  • Recall the verified safe-wide preset while Camera 4 is off program.
  • Recall the presets you expect to use and make sure they land where expected.
  • Try one slow diagonal. If the move collapses into mostly pan or mostly tilt, stop there and report FMP-8.
04 · Speeds
  1. Use conservative pan, tilt and zoom speeds. Camera 4 is not a whip-pan camera.
  2. Keep pan and tilt speeds reasonably close. A large mismatch can make a diagonal look like a single-axis move.
  3. Set preset speed so recalls arrive cleanly. Test every change off program.
  4. If a speed change does not behave the way you expect, stop changing settings and call video.
05 · Presets
  1. Recall the verified safe-wide preset first and confirm it lands correctly on the multiview.
  2. Recall each preset the show needs while Camera 4 is off program.
  3. If a preset lands wrong, do not keep calling it on air. Park the camera somewhere usable and tell video.
  4. Only SET or overwrite a preset when the Head of Video has released that preset number.
  5. If focus was changed manually, return the camera to the agreed focus mode before handing the shot back.

06 · Camera engineering

Leave the picture setup with video

The SuperJoy exposes camera controls that an operator does not normally need during the show.

  • You operate: framing, pan, tilt, zoom, focus and approved presets.
  • Video handles: exposure, white balance, gain, color, video format and camera/control configuration.
  • If the image looks dark, bright, off-color or otherwise wrong, call video and describe what you see.
  • Do not start changing engineering controls because the picture looks different from Cameras 1–3.
07 · Handoff

Say what the camera is doing

Keep the report simple: camera number, symptom, and whether you still have a usable shot.

“Camera 4, joystick stopped. Presets still work and I have the wide.”

“Camera 4, picture is up but it looks dark compared with the other cameras.”

03 / Show

Park wide. Move off program.

Open catwalk check-out →

For now, Camera 4 is preset-first. Have a usable wide ready before doors and make normal repositioning moves off program. Do not live-track performers until FMP-8 is cleared or the Head of Video specifically directs it.

Show operation

  1. Start from the verified safe wide.
  2. Use presets for normal show coverage and reposition while Camera 4 is off program.
  3. Until FMP-8 is cleared, do not follow performers live with the joystick unless the Head of Video directs it.
  4. Keep the OSD closed while the camera is on program.
  5. Watch 4 PTZ on the multiview.
  6. If control drops and the safe-wide preset was verified that day, recall it off program. If it was not verified, keep Camera 4 off air and establish a usable frame manually if control still works.
End of night
  1. Wait for the Head of Video to release the position.
  2. Park on the verified safe-wide preset if one was established for the show.
  3. Do not change engineering or controller configuration on the way out.
  4. Return the beltpack and headset.
  5. Report anything that behaved differently: what happened, roughly when, and whether the problem repeated.

04 / Known fault FMP-8

Two symptoms. Do not merge them.

FMP-8 is an engineering problem, not something the camera operator should solve during the show. If Camera 4 will not make a clean diagonal or a held move speeds up on its own, stop live joystick work and report the symptom. The tests below are for the video office on a load-in day or before doors.

Symptom A · reported September 11

A diagonal becomes pure pan or pure tilt

Symptom A test order
#TestWhat it separates
1Level PAN SPEED against TILT SPEED, then drive a diagonalAxis speed mismatch
2Drive a diagonal from the head's own web interface, with the Head of Video presentCamera from controller
3Check the BASIC MODE stateOne state disables the joystick
4Confirm the control protocol is VISCA over IP on the head's documented portProtocol mismatch
5Compare firmware at both endsVersion gap
6Check whether it follows preset recall or only joystick driveRecall path from drive path

Step 1 changes maximum velocity only. No SuperJoy response curve, acceleration, exponent or deadband setting has been identified, so it is not a curve adjustment and not a fix for symptom B.

Operator response

If the joystick move is not predictable, keep Camera 4 off program, return to a verified preset or usable parked frame, and call video. Do not change protocol, network, firmware or switcher settings during the show.

Symptom B · reported September 17

A held pan accelerates after several seconds

Do not read this as “the maximum pan speed is too high.” The behavior could live in the joystick response, the speed command sent, the head's zoom-adaptive pan and tilt speed, or the head's own motor acceleration. Separate them before adjusting anything.

Symptom B test order
#TestWhat it separates
1Hold the joystick at about 20 percent for 10 seconds without moving itSpeed rising with the stick still is time-based ramping. Run this first
2Repeat wide, then at maximum optical zoomWhether it depends on zoom
3Repeat with Tele Convert on, at about 40×Whether the crop changes the response
4Reduce PAN SPEED substantially and repeatCurve or ceiling; expect only the ceiling to move
5Drive the same move from the head's web interfaceCamera from controller
6Compare the current control method against VISCA over IPController or protocol
What good looks like

A small joystick move stays slow, more stick gives more speed, and a held stick does not continue accelerating on its own. If the response is not predictable, Camera 4 stays preset-first until the video office clears it.

05 / Focus and presets

Zoom tight, focus, compose wider.

Focus workflow

Set critical focus at maximum practical zoom on a high-contrast target, eyes or face where possible. Pull back to the intended composition, then recheck at long telephoto.

Do
  • Punch in to set focus, then compose out. Never judge critical focus from a wide frame.
  • Prefer manual focus for fixed positions where the shot is predictable.
  • Use a slightly wider frame when it does the job, rather than sitting at maximum zoom.
Watch for
  • Continuous autofocus during concert IMAG.
  • Haze, strobes and LED backgrounds, which make autofocus hunt.
  • Backlight, darkness, mic stands and performers crossing frame.

If focus is questionable, punch in and establish it. Do not guess from the wide. If the picture itself looks wrong rather than soft, call video instead of changing camera engineering.
