01 / Rules

Two controllers, two camera families.

Open catwalk check-in →

On every call, confirm which CAM button on the SuperJoy is the house head, the operator mode for the night, the safe wide preset, and who shades the PTZ picture with the Head of Video.

Not the camera control panel

The SuperJoy drives this head. The switcher's camera control panel shades the three URSA bodies over the SDI return and does not drive the PTZ. Its channel 4 reads 4 PTZ with type URSA_G2; that is a configuration artifact, not control of this head.

The switcher is an ATEM Television Studio HD8 ISO on software 9.0. The chassis has a remote-camera capability, but it is not driving this head, and nothing should be re-pointed at it without the Head of Video.

Stop conditions
  • Do not overwrite a stored preset, or SET a new one over an existing number, without the Head of Video.
  • Do not open the OSD while the camera is on program. The menu draws on the picture.
  • Do not change the head's address, control port, protocol or CAM ID, and do not factory reset it. A wrong address hides the camera on the isolated network.
  • Do not touch the camera control panel channel labeled 4 PTZ. It does not control this head.
  • Do not use the SuperJoy's HDMI OUTPUT as a confidence monitor. It only sees cameras.
  • Use P240 procedures only. There is no P200 in this building, so ignore P200 menu paths, DIP switch tables and reset sequences.
  • Do not go up to the catwalk head during a show unless the Head of Video releases you to it.

What is still open

  • Which CAM button selects the house head.
  • Whether input 4 is fed by the head's SDI tail or a network decoder.
  • PoE+ or 12 V DC power, and the exact mount position.
  • Which presets are stored, what they point at, and whether recall is reliable on this cross-vendor pairing.

The SuperJoy documents BirdDog as a supported brand, so the pairing is designed, not improvised.

02 / Before doors

Select, prove, set.

SuperJoy manual ↗
01 · Check in

Identify the assignment

  • The head is Camera 4 on switcher input 4. Record which CAM button selects it the first time you find out.
  • Operator mode for the night, agreed with the Head of Video.
  • Who shades the PTZ picture. The SuperJoy's left block is the only shading surface for this camera.
  • Default shot and any restricted angles from the catwalk.
  • Open faults: FMP-8, the follow fault.
  • Did the tour bring its own PTZ heads? A tour on September 12 placed two, downstage right and at front of house.
02 · Select

Power up and select the camera

  1. SuperJoy powered, LCD showing controller status.
  2. Press the CAM button for the house head. If the wrong camera answers, or none does, stop and check with the Head of Video before pressing CAM ID or SETUP.
  3. Confirm 4 PTZ shows a picture on the multiview. It is your only picture; there is no return at the catwalk.
  4. Press HOME. The head moves. If it does not, go to troubleshooting before touching any settings.
  5. Note the current PAN SPEED and TILT SPEED on the LCD before changing anything.
03 · Prove

One axis at a time

  • Pan left and right with the joystick. Smooth start and stop, no stall.
  • Tilt up and down. Same.
  • Zoom with the joystick twist, then with the WIDE/TELE dial.
  • Focus: AUTO FOCUS, then AUTO FOCUS LOCK and UNLOCK, then the NEAR/FAR dial in manual.
  • Drive a slow diagonal. If it reads as almost pure pan or pure tilt, that is FMP-8; run the speed test first.
  • Tally follows when the switcher takes input 4 to program.
04 · Speeds
  1. Level PAN SPEED against TILT SPEED with their buttons left of the LCD. A large mismatch reproduces the follow fault with no hardware fault present.
  2. Set ZOOM SPEED and FOCUS SPEED for the shot: slow for a live wide, faster for a move off program.
  3. Set PRESET SPEED so a recalled preset arrives without a visible lurch. Test it off program.
  4. Record the four values you land on so the next operator starts from the same place.
05 · Presets
  1. Recall the safe wide or home preset first with CALL and its number. Confirm it lands on the multiview.
  2. Recall each preset the show needs and confirm each lands. Presets are absolute positions; a bumped or power-cycled head can land elsewhere. Verify at doors, every show.
  3. To store: frame the shot, then SET and the number, only on a number the Head of Video has released to you.
  4. Hit AUTO FOCUS to save before you retake control. Skipping it leaves the head in the wrong state when you take the joystick back.
  5. Preset recall on this cross-vendor pairing is unproven. Do not build a library on it until recall is tested on a load-in day.

BASIC MODE cycles joystick and presets → presets only → limit off. One of those states disables the joystick entirely, which looks like a dead head. Check the mode before reporting a control fault.

07 · Picture
  1. Coordinate with the Head of Video before changing exposure or white balance. The URSAs are shaded at the control panel; this camera is shaded here, so matching it to Cameras 1–3 is a manual job.
  2. White balance: WHITE BALANCE CYCLE to the agreed mode, ONEPUSH TRIGGER on a neutral target under show light, then RED GAIN and BLUE GAIN only if told to.
  3. Exposure: EXPOSURE CYCLE to the agreed mode, then the IRIS/SHUTTER/GAIN knob within the agreed plan. BACKLIGHT only when the stage wash is behind the subject.
  4. Do not re-balance after the Head of Video has matched the cameras.

03 / Show

Park wide. Move off program.

Open catwalk check-out →

Park on the safe wide preset before doors. If control drops, CALL it first, then troubleshoot. A parked wide is a usable shot; a stalled diagonal is not.

Show operation

  1. Park on the safe wide preset before doors.
  2. Reposition only when off program unless directed. On program, moves are slow, deliberate and single-axis until FMP-8 is cleared.
  3. Keep the OSD closed on program.
  4. Watch the multiview tile for 4 PTZ, not the HDMI OUTPUT.
  5. Comms format: camera first, then symptom and impact. “Camera 4, lost joystick, presets still recall.”
  6. If control drops, CALL the safe wide preset first, then troubleshoot.
End of night
  1. Wait for the Head of Video to release the position.
  2. CALL the safe wide or home preset and leave the head parked there.
  3. Leave the speeds, mode and CAM button as found unless told otherwise, and record what you leave.
  4. Return the beltpack and headset.
  5. Report faults with the time, what was on program, the panel's mode, the speed values, and whether the fault reproduced.

04 / Known fault FMP-8

Two symptoms. Do not merge them.

Run these on a load-in day or before doors and record which step changed the behavior. Steps that touch the head's configuration need the Head of Video.

Symptom A · reported September 11

A diagonal becomes pure pan or pure tilt

Symptom A test order
#TestWhat it separates
1Level PAN SPEED against TILT SPEED, then drive a diagonalAxis speed mismatch
2Drive a diagonal from the head's own web interface, with the Head of Video presentCamera from controller
3Check the BASIC MODE stateOne state disables the joystick
4Confirm the control protocol is VISCA over IP on the head's documented portProtocol mismatch
5Compare firmware at both endsVersion gap
6Check whether it follows preset recall or only joystick driveRecall path from drive path

Step 1 changes maximum velocity only. No SuperJoy response curve, acceleration, exponent or deadband setting has been identified, so it is not a curve adjustment and not a fix for symptom B.

The switcher is not in this path

VISCA over IP arrived in ATEM software 9.7, and this switcher runs 9.0. That does not bear on FMP-8 as it stands: the SuperJoy talks to the head directly. It matters only if someone proposes moving PTZ control to the switcher, which is a Head of Video decision.

Symptom B · reported September 17

A held pan accelerates after several seconds

Do not read this as “the maximum pan speed is too high.” The behavior could live in the joystick response, the speed command sent, the head's zoom-adaptive pan and tilt speed, or the head's own motor acceleration. Separate them before adjusting anything.

Symptom B test order
#TestWhat it separates
1Hold the joystick at about 20 percent for 10 seconds without moving itSpeed rising with the stick still is time-based ramping. Run this first
2Repeat wide, then at maximum optical zoomWhether it depends on zoom
3Repeat with Tele Convert on, at about 40×Whether the crop changes the response
4Reduce PAN SPEED substantially and repeatCurve or ceiling; expect only the ceiling to move
5Drive the same move from the head's web interfaceCamera from controller
6Compare the current control method against VISCA over IPController or protocol
What good looks like

Position-dependent, not time-dependent. A small deflection crawls, more deflection goes faster, and a held deflection holds its speed. A fixed stick with rising speed is time-based ramping; disproportionate speed from more deflection is an aggressive response curve. They are different faults with different fixes.

05 / Focus and presets

Zoom tight, focus, compose wider.

Focus workflow

Set critical focus at maximum practical zoom on a high-contrast target, eyes or face where possible. Pull back to the intended composition, then recheck at long telephoto.

Do
  • Punch in to set focus, then compose out. Never judge critical focus from a wide frame.
  • Prefer manual focus for fixed positions where the shot is predictable.
  • Use a slightly wider frame when it does the job, rather than sitting at maximum zoom.
Watch for
  • Continuous autofocus during concert IMAG.
  • Haze, strobes and LED backgrounds, which make autofocus hunt.
  • Backlight, darkness, mic stands and performers crossing frame.

If focus is questionable, punch in and establish it. Do not guess from the wide. Tele Convert reach has not been checked under show lighting; verify it before relying on a 40× shot in a cue.

Open field checks

  • Photograph the head's model badge. Done: the plate reads P240.
  • Record which CAM button selects the house head.
  • Trace input 4: SDI tail from the head, or a network decoder ahead of the switcher.
  • Record head power, PoE+ or 12 V DC, and the mount position.
  • List the stored presets and what each points at.
  • Test preset recall on a load-in day before building the library.
  • Run the FMP-8 test orders and record the result.
  • Confirm whether a Camera 4 return has been run to the operator desk.
  • Record whether the head's own tally light is wired and follows program.
