The operators stay. The caller changes.

FMP camera positions are worked by Local operators on house nights and on most tour nights. Tours occasionally pay to bring their own operators and have ours shadow, but they usually defer to the house crew. This guide is built so a Local operator can walk in on any night, ask seven questions, fill one card, and cut clean for a director they met an hour ago.

Operator layer

Deliver for a caller you don't know

A new director will not know your position, your glass, or the room. They assume you will default somewhere useful. These rules hold on house and tour nights alike.

Core operator rules

RuleWhat it means on the stickWhy it survives any caller
Know your home, return to itAfter any call finishes, go back to your assigned subject without being askedThe director can always find you
Compose aheadOff tally, frame the next likely shot before the call comesUnfamiliar callers are often late; be ready early
Hold on tallyNo reframes on air unless slow and motivated. Let the performer move inside a stable frameA searching live frame reads as a production catching up
Offer contrastNever match the frame size and angle of the camera that is liveGives the director a real cut instead of a jump
Watch the musicAnticipate fills, solos, walks, entrances, and lighting hitsYou see the stage better than the video position does
Safe camera stays putIf you are named safe camera, you do not wander for a pretty shotThe one rule that saves a show with a new caller
Match the comms styleOffer shots on headset only if the director asked for offersSome callers want quiet channels; ask at the meeting
Focus and exposure are yoursStay sharp through zoom moves; flag exposure problems to shading off airNobody in the truck can fix a soft shot fast enough

Position discipline

FOHLong lens sticks

Sustained tight work

  • Best glass and most stable platform; usually the lead vocal
  • Frame, hold, follow naturally. No constant reframing
  • Watch headroom against stage height; aim near performer eye level when the platform allows
PitSC/SR and SL

Players and angles

  • Own a player, not the whole stage
  • Low angles on guitar and bass read strong on IMAG
  • Keep clear of security lanes and performer no go zones
  • Do not shoot into the drum riser from below; you get cymbals and a kick drum head
PTZPresets only

No live moves

  • The house PTZ will not drive pan and tilt together cleanly when following a mark
  • Recall presets only while off program, then trim small if needed
  • Tell every tour director this at the camera meeting
Tour addHandheld / shoulder

Motivated movement

  • Move for entrances, solos, reveals, or walking with the artist
  • Permission to hold; you do not need to move every second
  • Keep horizons intentional; never let two roaming cameras leave stage coverage together

Shadowing a tour operator (Mode C)

DoWhy
Hand over the house notes first: PTZ limits, cable paths, pit boundaries, IMAG wall layoutThe tour op knows the show, you know the room
Stay on headset and know the call style they are usingYou may be asked to take the position mid show

Before doors, every tour night

Seven questions, one card

Ask these at the camera meeting. If there is no meeting, grab the director on headset during checks. Fill the card for your position before doors.

Ask the director

#AskCatches
1How are you numbering us? By number or by position?Tour calling "Pit Left" while the house sheet says Cam 3
2What is my home, and who is your safe camera?Two operators both thinking they are free to roam
3"Ready, take" or just "take"?Missed cuts from mismatched calling habits
4Do you want shot offers on headset, or quiet?Comms clutter or missed opportunities
5IMAG notes: no shoot sides, crowd shots, restricted angles?An artist complaint after song one
6Any walk ons, B stage, guests, or pyro I am framing around?Blown reveals and unsafe positions
7Tour gear or house gear on my position?An unfamiliar body, lens, or zoom demand at the downbeat

Director layer

Calling the show

A strong concert cut preserves the performance so viewers always understand who is playing and why it matters, then adds energy without making the camera work the subject.

Core rules

RuleIn practice
Protect a safe shotFor IMAG, the safe shot is a lead vocal medium on FOH, not a stage wide. For a stream or record, add a wide safe as well. Do not take the safe camera tight unless the next two shots are composed
Assign ownershipOne camera owns lead vocal, others own players. A camera helps another only when its own home is covered
Cut on musical intentPhrase, lyric turn, fill, riff, entrance, lighting hit. Never mid action
Build contrastAlternate size, angle, static and moving, performer and audience. No near identical close up to close up
Hold shotsLet performers move inside a stable frame
Move selectivelyPush toward a chorus, pull out after a peak, moving camera for entrances and solos. Not every verse
Call earlyCall the next source before the current shot becomes unusable. An early clean cut beats a late reaction

Venue layer · FMP bowl

House camera plot

Three manned URSA positions plus one PTZ, cut on the house switcher and shaded at the video position. Output feeds IMAG on the two stage flanking walls and four lawn delay walls, and the venue TVs when the broadcast feed is taken.

Cam 1Pit SC/SR

Stage right player

  • URSA body
  • Home: guitar (standard plot)
  • Secondary: lead alternate angle
Cam 2FOH · Safe

Lead vocal

  • URSA body, Fujinon broadcast lens, viewfinder
  • Home: lead medium
  • Secondary: lead tight, full body on thrust walks
Cam 3Pit SL

Stage left player

  • URSA body
  • Home: bass (standard plot)
  • Secondary: keys when featured, lead from SL
PTZPresets only

Riser

  • Home: drums preset
  • Secondary: keys preset, band wide at intros
  • Never moved on air

Five piece band assignments

Assumes guitar SR, bass SL, drums and keys on the upstage riser. Swap Cam 1 and Cam 3 duties if the stage plot is mirrored.

SourceHomeSecondaryAvoid
Cam 2 FOHLead vocal medium (safe)Lead tight on key lyrics, full body on walksLeaving lead to chase a solo
Cam 1 Pit SC/SRGuitar, low angleLead alternate angle for chorus contrastMatching Cam 2's frame size on lead
Cam 3 Pit SLBassKeys when featured, lead from SLShooting up into the drum riser
PTZDrums presetKeys preset, band wide for intros and outrosAny on air move
The trade off Four sources for five players means someone rides a preset. Drums and keys go to the PTZ because their positions do not move. The cost: no drum coverage while the PTZ is on a band wide, so call the wide at intros and outros only and keep drums as the resting preset.

The practical test Before calling any cut, name what the viewer learns from the next shot that the current one does not show. Before offering any shot, make sure it answers that question.
